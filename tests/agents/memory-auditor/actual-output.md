  actively-referenced KI with one missing field.

  Worth a human check on whether the project-scoped path has looser authoring — one sample is not a pattern,
  but if project KIs are created by a different route than portable ones, that route may be skipping schema
  validation.

- [`shared/knowledge/ki-redis-patterns.md`] + [`shared/knowledge/ki-redis-sliding-window.md`]: both cover
  Redis rate-limiting technique. The snapshot describes the second as "**nearly identical content to
  ki-redis-patterns.md**", and the pair share the `infrastructure` domain and the `redis` tag.

  This is a **strong overlap candidate and a genuinely ambiguous one**, which is why it is reported for a
  human or `memory-engineer` decision rather than resolved here. The two plausible resolutions differ in
  kind:
  - If `ki-redis-patterns` is a **broad Redis-techniques document** that happens to contain a sliding-window
    section, then `ki-redis-sliding-window` is the more focused and more useful artifact, and the right move
    is to trim the section from the general document and cross-reference. Merging *into* the general one
    would make the specific knowledge harder to find.
  - If `ki-redis-patterns` is **specifically about rate limiting** under a vague name, the two are true
    duplicates and one should absorb the other.

  The snapshot does not contain enough of either body to distinguish these, and the difference determines
  which file survives. **Do not merge on the basis of this audit.**

  One asymmetry that should inform the decision: `ki-redis-sliding-window` was referenced on 2026-07-15,
  while `ki-redis-patterns` has not been referenced since 2026-01-10 and has no incoming links (see below).
  The specific KI is the one that is actually being used. That is evidence about which name people reach
  for, and it points the same direction as the first resolution above.

  **Not overlapping, for contrast**: `ki-redis-cache-invalidation` also carries the `redis` tag and the
  `infrastructure` domain, but covers TTL and event-driven cache invalidation — a different subject that
  shares a technology. It is correctly a separate KI, and flagging it would be a false positive driven by
  tag co-occurrence rather than content.
